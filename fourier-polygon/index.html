Polygons from Fourier data

An N-gon with vertices v0, …, vN−1 expressed as complex numbers can be written in terms of discrete Fourier coefficients vj = V0 + V1ωj + Σk=2N−1 Vk ωjk, where ω = e2πi/N is a primitive Nth root of unity and V0, …, VN−1 are complex numbers, called Fourier coefficients. Adjusting V0 merely translates the polygon in the plane, and multiplying each Vk by the same complex number α merely rotates and scales the polygon. This means that the underlying shape of the polygon can be described using just N − 2 complex numbers z2, …, zN−1, where zk = Vk/V1. The inverse discrete Fourier transform of [0, 1, z2, …, zN−1] then gives the vertices of a similar polygon, balanced around the origin. When z2 = ⋯ = zN−1 = 0, this produces the regular N-gon.
